\paragraph{Languages and pairs}
BTOR2's interpreter is hardware's root judge; C's is the
preprocessor-free ILP32 integer fragment SV-COMP's loop and
bit-vector tasks are written in, total and deterministic; RISC-V's is
the middle vertex, anchored by no domain. The three pairs among
them, \code{c--btor2}, \code{c--riscv}, \code{riscv--btor2}, each
carry programs, witnesses, observables, and claims, with mutants per
artifact kind and exchange rates of 3.65, 5.37, and 3.13 target bytes
per source byte; \code{c--btor2@2} also carries certificates, and
C's \code{induction} checker builds its machine by the route through
RISC-V,
so a proof that travelled the pair is certified by an encoding the
pair never touched. The
reverse edge \code{btor2--c} is the one pair on the frame contract of
\cref{sec:pairs}: every state a global of the smallest unsigned
container holding its width, one statement per node, \code{ite}
branch-free, a \code{bad} at machine frame $t$ observed at C frame
$n_0 + n \cdot t + \textit{bad}$; seventeen corpus machines, six
certificates carried both ways, thirteen mutants refused, 4.19 bytes
per byte; array sorts, widths above 64, and non-constant initial
values refused loudly.

\paragraph{Certificate forms and searches}
BTOR2 judges \code{induction} (bit-invariants and k-induction) and
\code{clauses} (a conjunction of clauses over state bits, as
property-directed searches converge to); C judges \code{induction}
and \code{ranges} (per program point a box over the scalar slots,
unlisted points asserted unreachable). At BTOR2 live
\code{btor2-sim@4} (explicit-state enumeration, guided sampling when
the input space is large, arrays as sparse maps), \code{btor2-bmc}
(bit-blasting per frame to an and-inverter graph decided by a
generated CDCL solver, an accelerator in C admitted by
byte-agreement), \code{btor2-ind@3}, \code{btor2-ic3@2},
and \code{btor2-pdr} \citep{een2011pdr}, written from nothing it
shares with \code{btor2-ic3} --- its own parser, bit-blaster, and SAT
solver --- so that its lineage is disjoint from every earlier
search's; at C lives \code{c-ai@2}, interval abstract interpretation
that writes a \code{ranges} certificate or an honest partial and
never a bare claim.

\paragraph{Revisions}
Fifteen language entries, five pair entries, thirteen search entries,
and two domains stand in the tree; a name binds to its highest
revision, and the earlier revisions are the system's history without
an edit: \code{btor2-ind@2} and \code{btor2-ic3@2} blasted the signed
division family after thirteen SV-COMP programs and four HWMCC
machines crashed the first revisions; \code{btor2@6}, \code{c@6},
\code{c@7}, and \code{riscv@2} add vectors and controls alone after
the measurement of \cref{sec:faults}, and change no line of any
judge.

\paragraph{The generator}
% Confirmed by the author, 2026-10-05; the tree itself records
% lineage tags, not sessions or prompts.
Every implementation in the registry, and the kernel, was written by
language-model agents at the command line the loop describes
(\cref{sec:frontier}); the entries declare four generator tags among
their lineages. A human chose the benchmarks, set the goal of each
campaign, and pulled the plug. Two things the tree does not record
limit what can be said about the generator: an entry that fails the
gate leaves no trace, so the rate at which candidates were refused
during development is not known; and nothing was generated twice
from one instruction, so the generator's variance is not measured.
\Cref{sec:faults} measures what can be measured after the fact.
