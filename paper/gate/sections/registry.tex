\paragraph{The triangle}
The hardware domain entered first, before its root interpreter
existed: root \code{btor2}, with the HWMCC'24 bit-vector track and
the official verdicts of its solvers as recorded testimony. Then the
BTOR2 interpreter, the trusted floor; the naive first citizen,
\code{btor2-sim}, exhaustive enumeration with fixpoint detection when
the free-input space is small and guided sampling otherwise; and
\code{btor2-bmc}, bit-blasting per frame to an and-inverter graph
decided by a generated CDCL SAT solver, every model replayed before
it is emitted, with an accelerator in C admitted by byte-agreement on
its corpus. The software domain and the C interpreter followed ---
the preprocessor-free ILP32 integer fragment SV-COMP's loop and
bit-vector tasks are written in, its semantics total and
deterministic --- then RISC-V as the middle vertex, anchored by no
domain, and the three pairs \code{c--btor2}, \code{c--riscv},
\code{riscv--btor2}, each carrying programs, witnesses, observables,
and claims, with mutants per artifact kind and exchange rates of
3.65, 5.37, and 3.13 target bytes per source byte. Two certificate
forms then arrived on BTOR2 by revision of the language ---
\code{induction} (bit-invariants and k-induction) and \code{clauses}
(a conjunction of clauses over state bits, as property-directed
searches converge to) --- with the searches that write them,
\code{btor2-ind} and \code{btor2-ic3}; then C's own \code{induction}
checker and the certificate carry-back of the C-to-BTOR2 pair, by
revision; and a further revision of C's checker that builds its
machine by the RISC-V road, sharing no text with the C-to-BTOR2
encoding, so that a proof which travelled that pair is certified by
an encoding the pair never touched.

\paragraph{The reverse edge}
C gained a third certificate form, \code{ranges} --- per program
point a box over the scalar slots, unlisted points asserted
unreachable --- and its first search, \code{c-ai}, interval abstract
interpretation that writes such a certificate or an honest partial
and never a bare claim. On the frame contract of \cref{sec:pairs}
the pair \code{btor2--c} was admitted with all five channels: every
state a global of the smallest unsigned container holding its width,
one statement per node, \code{ite} branch-free, so that a \code{bad}
at machine frame $t$ is observed at C frame $n_0 + n \cdot t +
\textit{bad}$. Seventeen corpus machines, six certificates carried
both ways, thirteen mutants refused, 4.19 bytes per byte; array
sorts, widths above 64, and non-constant initial values are refused
loudly.

\paragraph{Three more searches}
\code{btor2-sim@4} carries arrays as sparse maps whose cells are
drawn as they are read. \code{btor2-pdr} is a second
property-directed search \citep{een2011pdr} written from nothing it
shares with \code{btor2-ic3} --- its own parser, bit-blaster, and SAT
solver --- so that its lineage is disjoint from every earlier
search's; seven corpus machines, three certificates discharged, three
mutants refused. \code{c-ai@2} reads the reverse edge's branch-free
\code{ite} as the selection it is and adds a case split.

\paragraph{Revisions that add only vectors}
An admitted entry is never edited, and a revision need not change a
line of code. After the measurement of \cref{sec:faults} each
language was revised with vectors and controls alone ---
\code{btor2@6}, \code{c@6}, \code{c@7}, \code{riscv@2} ---
\cref{sec:closing} says with what. Fifteen language entries, five
pair entries, thirteen search entries, and two domains stand in the
tree; a name binds to its highest revision, and the earlier revisions
tell the system's small history without editing anything:
\code{btor2-ind@2} and \code{btor2-ic3@2} blasted the signed division
family after thirteen SV-COMP programs and four HWMCC machines
crashed the first revisions, each byte-identical to its predecessor
on the predecessor's corpus.

\paragraph{The generator}

Every implementation in the registry, and the kernel, was written by
language-model agents working at the command line the loop describes
(\cref{sec:intro}, Provenance); the entries declare four generator
tags among their lineages. A human chose the benchmarks, set the goal
of each campaign, and pulled the plug. Two things the tree does not record
limit what can be said about the generator itself: an entry that
fails the gate leaves no stamp and no trace, so the rate at which
generated candidates were refused during development is not known;
and nothing was generated twice from the same instruction, so the
variance of the generator is not measured. \Cref{sec:faults}
measures what can be measured after the fact: how the gate and the
grades respond to faults placed deliberately.
