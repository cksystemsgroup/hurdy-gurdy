\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Verification infrastructure is now being written by language models:
interpreters, translators, invariant generators, proof scripts, and
the checkers that are supposed to catch the rest, each text produced
by a process that cannot be inspected and is wrong at an unknown
rate. Two answers are current. One verifies the artifact: repair the proof until
a checker accepts it, or generate the code together with a
specification and a proof \citep{first2023baldur,sun2024clover}. The
other distrusts the artifact and checks its outputs: certifying
algorithms \citep{mcconnell2011certifying}, proof-carrying code
\citep{necula1997pcc}, validated verification witnesses
\citep{beyer2015witnesses}. Both are about one artifact at a time.
Neither says what happens when the checker is generated too, when the
translation that carried the program to the checker is generated,
when the map that carries the verdict back is generated, and when the
search that produced the evidence was generated last week by the same
process.

This paper describes a system built on the premise that every one of
those pieces is untrusted, and judged whole. It started from an empty
repository with a specification, and everything that has entered
since --- every interpreter, translator, search, checker, and
carry-back --- is Python written by a language model and admitted
through one gate as an \emph{entry} of the \emph{registry}. The separation that organizes it is one sentence:
\textbf{generation produces syntax; only interpretation produces
truth.} A \emph{language} is a syntax with an \emph{interpreter} and,
per declared \emph{certificate form}, a \emph{checker}; these are the
\emph{judges}, and the trusted base is exactly the admitted judges, a
list the kernel prints. Every other executable is a \emph{transport}
--- translator, carry-back, or budgeted search --- untrusted whatever
it computes. A question lives at a language; a \emph{route} carries it across \emph{pairs} of
languages to a search; whatever comes back is judged where it lands,
and the \emph{gap} of a result is the number of hops between the
question and the last judgment its evidence passed
(\cref{fig:routes}). Gap zero is \grade{certified}: a witness
replayed, or a certificate discharged, by the \emph{home} judge, the
judge of the language the question lives in. A positive
gap is \grade{checked}. No judgment is \grade{claimed}. The
\emph{residual} --- what a result still rests on --- is the
\emph{lineages}, the declared descent, of the hops inside the gap plus
the judge that ran; every judgment
removes everything upstream of it (\cref{thm:residual}, proved in
Lean).

A design like this makes two claims that can be wrong. The first is
about transports: a faulty translator, carry-back, or search can cost
answers but can never forge a \grade{certified} one, and whatever it
corrupts says so in its grade and names it in its residual. The
second is about judges: the trusted base is small enough, and tested
well enough, to be believed. We test both by fault injection
(\cref{sec:faults}). For the first we mutate every transport in the
registry, \emph{remove the gate}, force each mutant in, and play it
on questions whose answer is known: 16,720 plays of 1,672 mutants
yield 133 wrong answers, 127 \grade{claimed}, 6 \grade{checked} at
gap one, none \grade{certified}, every one naming the faulty entry in
its residual. For the second we mutate the judges: half of 700 are
refused at the gate, the survivors concentrate in the certificate
checkers, and a differential probe finds six of them laxer than the
checker they mutate --- real holes, closed without touching a judge,
by revisions that add test vectors whose expected values are
confirmed by tools that never enter the system.

The boards are the third measurement (\cref{sec:boards}). Two domains
have entered --- hardware, rooted at BTOR2 \citep{niemetz2018btor2},
and software, rooted at a fragment of C --- with RISC-V between them,
anchored by no domain. Nothing in the kernel is specific to either:
a domain enters by one pair to a hub where searches live, and every
search there then reaches its questions, which is how a software
question is answered by a hardware search and how a domain of
mathematics or physics would enter (\cref{sec:conclusion}). Four
generated pairs connect the three
languages, six generated searches live at BTOR2 and C, and four
benchmarks pinned from HWMCC'24 --- three rungs: small, middle,
arrays --- and SV-COMP 2025 stand at 48 of 74,
28 of 79, 47 of 80, and 12 of 55 questions settled, 49 universal
answers \grade{certified} at gap zero, no contradiction. The
generated searches are not competitive with the solvers whose
verdicts sit beside the boards; \cref{sec:performance} says why
performance was not pursued first and draws the column the design
reserves for it --- the competitions' results and a calibration of
their solvers on the playing host beside each board --- and \cref{sec:frontier} says how the frontier is pushed --- the
turns that produced the tree are in \cref{tab:moves}.

\paragraph{AI Use}
All of the system --- the kernel, its tests, the second lineage of
its pure half, the Lean development, every entry in the registry ---
and the instruments of \cref{sec:measurements} --- the
fault-injection harness, the generators of test-vector expectations
--- are code generated by language-model agents (Anthropic's Claude
Fable, through the Claude Code command-line agent). Most of this
paper's text, its tables, and its figures are generated by the same
agents from the specification and the measurement records. No human
reviewed the code or the text for semantic correctness. The human
contribution is the architecture --- the separation of judges from
transports, the gate, the decision that grades are computed and never
asserted --- and the choices of what to build, measure, and claim.
No number here originates with a model: each is read from logs and
stamps the kernel wrote, the boards and the fault-injection counts
regenerate from the deposited records by one command each, the proofs
are checked by Lean, and the test vectors' expected values were
confirmed by tools that are not generative.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
\item A design in which every executable of a verification system is
  untrusted generated text and one gate admits all of them: two
  kinds, three judgments an artifact faces on crossing a pair (the
  square, replay, discharge), two crossings nothing judges
  (\cref{sec:design}).
\item Grades as geometry: gap, grade, and residual computed from the
  log, the order on results and the residual's properties proved
  (\cref{sec:grades,sec:kernel}); a kernel outside the gate made
  solid four ways (\cref{sec:kernel}).
\item The loop as an operating procedure --- the commands, what an
  entry ships, what the frontier tells the player, the conjecture
  order --- and the record of its turns (\cref{sec:frontier}).
\item A fault-injection method for such systems and its results: the
  no-forge claim tested with the gate removed, the trusted base's
  exposure measured and localized, six holes found and closed with
  outside ground truth (\cref{sec:faults,sec:closing}).
\item Measurements at one frozen commit, every number tied to a file
  in the tree, the negatives reported as results (\cref{sec:boards}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What is not claimed}
The square is checked empirically, per program, on a pair's corpus;
per-program translation correctness is not proved. The seal around
generated executables (\cref{sec:generation}) is a blank \code{PATH} and an emptied
environment, measured by tests, not a proof. Half the sampled judge
mutants survived admission and a third still do after the revisions;
for most of those we can say only that no certificate we could find
distinguishes them from the judge. The \emph{corroborated} flag ---
results of disjoint lineage agreeing --- has fired only between two
searches, never between two judges. The
boards are not ranked against any solver; the competitions' results
and one host calibration are recorded beside each question as
testimony, and the one pass played against them is a budget move.
