\section{Measurements}
\label{sec:measurements}

Every number in this section is read from the tree at tag
\code{era6-campaign-2}: the boards \code{runs/*/frontier.md}, which
regenerate byte-identically from the logs \code{runs/*/log.jsonl};
the pinned benchmarks \code{runs/*/benchmark.json}, whose
\code{official} field records per question the verdict the
competition archive published; and the stamps in the registry.
Nothing is reported that is not in the log.

\paragraph{Protocol}
Four benchmarks are pinned, every program by sha256
(\cref{tab:benchmarks}). Three are tiers of the HWMCC'24 bit-vector
track \citep{hwmcc24,niemetz2018btor2} selected in the fifth
generation and carried forward: \code{hwmcc24-mini} (74 machines,
23\% of the track), \code{hwmcc24-arrays} (55 machines with array
sorts), and \code{hwmcc24-mid} (80). The fourth,
\code{svcomp25-mini}, holds 79 ReachSafety loop and bit-vector tasks
from SV-COMP 2025 \citep{beyer2021svcomp}, 31 of them tasks that at
most three of the verifiers in the archive answered correctly.
% TODO cite: the SV-COMP 2025 competition report (TACAS 2025), if the
% author confirms the reference; beyer2021svcomp is the series entry
% already in references.bib.
An ask is \code{exists bad} at a bound, or \code{forall bad} at a
bound or at $\infty$. A play runs every question along every admitted
route whose fragment admits it, one wall per route: 60\,s on hardware
throughout; on software 30\,s for the first campaign's full plays
(its very first play, over the fourteen hardest tasks alone, ran at
60\,s) and 60\,s since; a search that runs to twice its wall is
killed and booked as a partial saying so. The player is the driver's
\code{play}; later passes used \code{--via} (only the routes an entry
opens) and \code{--only} (a named subset of questions), alone or
together, and the log records which.

\subsection{The first campaign (2026-08-30)}
\label{sec:campaign}

The hardware board closed at 43 of 74 after four plays (the fourth,
with every search at its revised entry, moved nothing): 14
universal answers \grade{certified} at gap 0 --- thirteen
\code{induction} certificates written by \code{btor2-ind} and one
clause invariant written by \code{btor2-ic3}, each discharged by
BTOR2's own judge, so that the search that found it is no longer in
its residual --- 9 witnesses certified by replay (four found by
bounded model checking, three by the naive enumeration, two by the
inductive search's bounded phase), and 20 bounded claims covering
their asks, two of them $\textit{all}(\infty)$ bare claims from the
naive search's enumeration to a fixpoint, graded \grade{claimed}
because a fixpoint the search reports is not a certificate the judge
can check. The residual of every certified answer on the board reads
\code{btor2-cert-g1 btor2-interp-g1} and the two generator tags ---
the judge and nothing else.

The software board closed at 26 of 79: 14 witnesses
\grade{certified} at C, twelve of which walked home across both
bridges of the triangle --- found at BTOR2 on the route C to RISC-V
to BTOR2 and replayed by $I_C$ --- and two across C to BTOR2 with the
naive search; three universal answers first \grade{checked} at gap 1
(k-induction certificates with $k = 7, 6, 3$, discharged by BTOR2's
judge, their residual naming the C-to-BTOR2 pair) and then, once C
shipped its own \code{induction} checker and the pair gained a
certificate carry-back by revision, lifted to \grade{certified} at
gap 0 by \code{regrade} alone, without re-solving anything, in 0.55,
0.41, and 0.51\,s; then, when C's judge was revised to build its
machine by the RISC-V road, re-judged again by \code{regrade} (0.82,
0.42, 0.58\,s) so that their residual no longer names the route the
evidence travelled; then a fourth proof, about a C program with a
2048-element array, found k-inductive at BTOR2, discharged there,
carried home, and certified at C with every array a state of its
own; and eight bounded claims covering an ask of 100. No
contradiction anywhere. No \emph{corroborated} flag either, and
honestly so: every route shares the C front end and one generator,
and the flag is reserved for disjoint descent.

\subsection{The reverse edge (2026-09-07)}
\label{sec:reverse}

The pair \code{btor2--c} of \cref{sec:registry} opens one new route
on every hardware question: BTOR2 to C to \code{c-ai}. Of
\code{hwmcc24-mini}'s 74 machines, 57 translate and agree with
BTOR2's own interpreter on random stimuli; 17 are refused at widths
above 64, revision 1's fragment. Played through the new route alone,
every one of the 74 routes came back a partial, and the log says why:
on 41 machines, intervals see \code{ERR} reachable, because the
pinned branch-free \code{ite} encoding --- $b \oplus ((a \oplus b)
\wedge -c)$ --- is opaque to a box; on 4, the iteration budget was
spent before a fixpoint; 12 are existential questions, which a
proving search never refutes; 17 are the refused translations. On
\code{svcomp25-mini}, \code{c-ai} certified four universal answers at
gap 0 in 0.038\,s of search each --- \code{safe1}, \code{safe6}, and
\code{safe21}, already certified by the BTOR2 route and now shown
under \code{c-ai} because the key is equal and the latest
adjudication wins, and \code{safe14}, previously settled by a bare
claim covering its ask of 100 and now $\textit{all}(\infty)$
\grade{certified} --- and returned a partial on the other 75: 38 with
\code{ERR} reachable in the abstraction, 37 existential. \textbf{Both
boards stayed where they were: 43 of 74 and 26 of 79.} That is the
measurement the edge was built to make: the bridge is judged and open
in both directions, and what a word-level search buys on hardware
questions is bounded by what it can see through masks. The next
search at C moves the board, or does not, on an edge that no longer
needs building.

\subsection{The two pinned rungs, played for the first time (2026-09-07)}
\label{sec:rungs}

\code{hwmcc24-arrays} settled 1 of 55: one witness at depth 6, found
by bounded model checking in 0.5\,s and again by the inductive
search, certified by replay; and 99 bounded claims (50 by
\code{btor2-bmc}, 49 by \code{btor2-ind}) that cover none of their
asks --- 38 of the 55 questions ask $\forall$ at $\infty$, and the
deepest bound reached is 66. The reason is structural, and the log
names it on every row: the explicit-state search and IC3 refuse array
sorts (55 partials each, \code{unsupported sort: array}); the reverse
edge refuses all 55; four machines with nested arrays lie outside the
bounded searches' fragment and are refused by both; and one inductive
run was killed at twice the wall. On this rung only bounded model
checking and k-induction run at all, and k-induction found no
inductive strengthening on any of the 49 machines it ran to a bound
on. Officially, 38 of the 54 open questions were solved by none of
the five solvers in the archive; the other 16 were.

\code{hwmcc24-mid} settled 47 of 80: 12 universal answers
\grade{certified} at gap 0 by the inductive search, 8 witnesses
certified by replay (six by bounded model checking, one by
enumeration, one by the inductive search), and 27 bounded claims
covering their asks (21 by k-induction's bounded phase, 6 by bounded
model checking): 26 answer $\forall$ at 20, and one, \code{unsafe3},
answers an existential ask at 30 in the negative --- no failure
within 30 frames, three searches agreeing, nothing judging them. Of
the 33 open, the log's partials say what
was spent: the explicit-state search reports \code{free bits exceed
enumeration} on 79 of 80 machines; the interval route spent its
iteration budget before a fixpoint on 35 and was refused translation
on 23; 21 runs were killed at twice the wall (14 inductive, 4
interval, 2 bounded, 1 IC3); and six runs --- three inductive, three
IC3 --- ended in a search failure with exit code 1, booked as
partials. Officially, the 8 open universal questions at $\infty$ were
solved by none of the eight solvers; the 17 open bounded and 8 open
existential questions were.

\subsection{Ten minutes of IC3 (2026-09-07)}
\label{sec:tenminutes}

The ten open universal questions of \code{hwmcc24-mini} --- seven
officially unsolved, three officially unsat at $\infty$ --- were
played through \code{btor2-ic3} alone with a wall of 600\,s
(\cref{tab:tenminutes}). One proof: \code{open5}, a 64-bit machine
officially unsat for all eight solvers within 4\,s, proved by a
clause invariant in 30.2\,s once the budget let the search go deep
enough, discharged by \code{btor2:clauses}, \grade{certified} at gap
0. Three bounded claims written, of which one enlarged the board's
best bound (\code{open6}, from 133 to 177) and two matched the
incumbent's 300. Six runs killed at twice the wall with nothing to
show. \code{hwmcc24-mini} stands at 44 of 74. Ten times the clock
bought one question: the board is search-limited, not
budget-limited, and what moves it next is a search that reasons
differently, not a longer wall.

\subsection{Against the official verdicts}
\label{sec:official}

The competitions' verdicts are anchors: recorded per question with
provenance, drawn beside the board, never ranked, never a judge. Read
that way, the four boards say the following. On \code{hwmcc24-mini},
all 44 settled questions carry an official verdict that agrees with
the board's; of the 30 open, 7 were solved by no official solver and
23 were --- 15 bounded safety questions on which the bounded searches
reach depths of 2 to 17 against an ask of 20, two safety questions at
$\infty$ bounded at 300 and 239, and 6 existential questions at bound
30 whose witnesses the searches did not find. On
\code{svcomp25-mini}, every one of the 79 tasks was solved by at
least one verifier, and every one of the 26 settled here agrees with
its label; the 53 open include all 31 tasks selected because at most
three verifiers answered them, none of which any route here settles
--- the bounded searches reach depths of 11 to 300 on them, all below
$\infty$. On \code{hwmcc24-mid}, the 8 open universal questions at
$\infty$ are exactly the 8 no official solver answered; the one
existential question settled in the negative, \code{unsafe3}, is
recorded sat by five solvers at a depth the archive does not publish
--- a bounded bare claim beside an unbounded verdict, which is not a
contradiction and is the one settled row on any board whose official
verdict is not the same answer. On \code{hwmcc24-arrays}, the one
settled question agrees with its label, and 38 of the 54 open were
solved by no official solver. No board holds a replayed witness or a
discharged certificate whose official verdict disagrees with it, and
no board holds a question settled here that the archive records as
unsolved.

\subsection{What the numbers say}
\label{sec:reading}

Three readings are supported by the log, and no more. First, the
geometry does what it was built to do: 32 universal answers across
the boards are \grade{certified} at gap 0, and for every one of them
the residual printed on the board is the home judge and nothing else
--- the search, and on the software board the route, are gone from
what the answer rests on; the regrade of three C proofs moved them
from gap 1 to gap 0 in under a second each without re-solving, and
re-judged them again under a judge of different descent. Second, the
honesty rules bite where they should: the 20, 7, and 27 covering
claims on the three boards are \grade{claimed}, and the two
$\textit{all}(\infty)$ answers from the naive search are
\grade{claimed} despite being the strongest value on their questions,
because nothing judged them. Third, the negatives are structural, not
budgetary: the reverse edge is blind to the \code{ite} idiom, the
array rung is refused by three of five searches, and ten minutes of
IC3 bought one question. The next moves the tree names --- a
property-directed search with proper generalization at BTOR2, an
explicit-state search that accepts arrays, an interval analysis that
reads the \code{ite} idiom --- are (a)-moves of the conjecture order,
and the boards will say whether they move.
