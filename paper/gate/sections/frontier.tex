\section{Pushing the frontier}
\label{sec:frontier}

The frontier of a benchmark is pushed by one loop (\cref{fig:loop}),
run by a player who never writes a result: a language model, or a
human at the same command line. This section is the loop as an
operating procedure, and the record of the turns that produced the
tree at the tag.

\paragraph{What an entry ships}
An entry is a directory the player writes and \code{admit}
adjudicates. A search ships \code{manifest.json} (kind, language,
name, revision, predecessor hash, lineage, the observables it
targets), \code{solve.py}, an optional trust-inert \code{ledger.py},
a \code{corpus/} of programs with asks whose evidence must judge
valid, and \code{controls/} of mutants that must fail ---
\code{btor2-sim@4} ships seven corpus questions and three mutants:
one drops array cells, one lies, one overclaims. A pair ships
\code{T.py}, the carry-backs it declares
(\code{lam\_wit.py}, \code{lam\_cert.py}, on the frame contract
\code{lam\_in.py} and \code{lam\_bound.py}), its direction, kept
observables, and corpus, with mutants per carried artifact. A
language ships its interpreter, vectors with pinned observables,
mutants, and under \code{evidence/<form>/} a checker with example
certificates and mutant certificates. The gate stamps what it checked
into the manifest's \code{admission} field, and the stamp is the only
self-description an entry is allowed.

Three rows of \code{hwmcc24-arrays} say what the frontier is made of.
The first is a bare claim to bound 6 on an unbounded ask, settled by

printer at BTOR2, or a checker for a form some search already writes.

its trust column holds only BTOR2's judges. The frontier's second
reading is structural: which roots reach which searches, and the one
pair each unreached search is missing.

\paragraph{The conjecture order}
Conjecture in this order --- semantics first, then syntax: (a)
\emph{new judging and searching at existing languages} --- a search,
a certificate form with its checker, an accelerator for a proven
bottleneck; (b) \emph{new transports} --- pairs, and carry-backs
retrofitted onto admitted pairs by revision; two (b)-moves are
canonical, a certificate carry-back added to a proven translator, and
a translator from a root that reaches no hub to the nearest one,
which is the entry move of every new domain and inherits every search
at the hub for the price of one translator; (c) \emph{new languages},
only when a translation or solving move keeps winning ad hoc. Then
generate, admit, re-play the routes the entry opens. The kernel ships with zero
languages, transports, and domains; presented a benchmark, the first
admissions are the domain --- root name plus anchors --- then the
root's interpreter, then a naive search in pure Python. Witnesses
need no declared form, so existential questions settle
\grade{certified} with a single admitted interpreter; the first
checker arrives when universal questions need better than
\grade{claimed}. \Cref{tab:moves} is the record that this is how the
tree was populated, and \cref{fig:movement} is what each turn did to
the boards.

\paragraph{Why the searches are not competitive, yet}
\label{sec:performance}
The generated searches implement bounded model checking, k-induction,
IC3, PDR, and interval abstract interpretation in Python, each with
its own generated SAT solver; the solvers whose verdicts sit beside
the boards embed decades of tuning, and the generation rule
(\cref{sec:generation}) forbids wrapping them. Three facts about the
design fixed the order in which things were asked of this commit: the
frontier is a function of level, bound, grade, and gap, never of
cost, so a faster search moves no question unless it also settles or
regrades one; every performance move the design admits is judged ---
an accelerator by byte-agreement, a hint by the judgment its output
faces, a regrade by the checker --- so performance can be pursued
after the trust claims are measured, by the same loop, with no
revision to anything the fault injection examined; and that
measurement had to come first, because its truth set is the intact
judges and the boards' certified answers, and a board of fast bare
claims would have measured nothing.

\paragraph{The oracle column}
What the design reserves for a comparison is testimony: the
per-question results a competition archive publishes are pinned
beside its verdicts, one calibration run of a pinned solver on the
playing host makes the ratio honest, and the board draws each
question's cost against the best oracle's --- recorded, never ranked,
never a grade. \Cref{tab:oracle} draws it at the tag, with the one
budget move made against it: the 39 open existential questions the
competitions solved, played through \code{btor2-bmc} alone with a
900\,s wall under a guard that kills a search above 6\,GB on a
17\,GB host (the competitions allowed 120 and 15). Where a question
is settled, the generated search cost a median of 2 to 42 times the
best official solver's time. The pass settled five of the 39 --- four
witnesses certified by replay and one covering claim --- and on those
the pinned solver on the same host, with the same bound, is 6 to 14
times faster. What ended the other routes is named per route, and it
is not the wall: a conflict budget that derives from the declared
wall at the reference's pace and that the C accelerator spends in
300\,s; memory, on the middle rung and on software; a ceiling of 300
frames for unbounded asks, one CFG node per frame; and the structural
ceilings of the eager array reduction. The host calibration reads
the ratio: with the question's bound, \code{btormc} answers 35 of the
46 existential hardware questions within 900\,s on this host and 6 of
the 16 the pass played, and \code{cbmc} with doubling unwindings
answers 20 of the 37 software ones and 6 of the 23 the pass played. Two bounded claims
sit beside unbounded verdicts, one on each hardware rung, and
\code{btormc} confirms within 5\,s for both that the bad state lies
beyond the pinned bound 30. The column names the
next (a)-moves as the frontier names the rest: a budget calibrated at
admission to the accelerator's measured pace, a deeper ceiling with
one basic block per frame, a lazy array reduction, eleven cones above
\code{btor2-pdr}'s cap, seventeen translations refused above 64 bits.
