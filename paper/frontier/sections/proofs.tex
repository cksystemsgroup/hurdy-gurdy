\section{What is proved, and what became of the theorems}
\label{sec:proofs}

\paragraph{The kernel's own development}
The kernel is the only hand-written code --- five stdlib-only Python
modules, under nine hundred lines --- and its properties are
mechanized in a dedicated, dependency-free Lean~4 development
(\texttt{kernel/mechanization/}), separate from the previous
calculus's development, which remains the record of the old model:

\begin{itemize}
\item the result order on keys $(\textit{level}, \textit{bound},
  \textit{grade})$ is a strict order
  (\textsf{Key.lt\_irrefl}, \textsf{Key.lt\_trans},
  \textsf{Key.lt\_asymm} --- axiom-free);
\item best-per-question, a fold over the log, is monotone under
  append (\textsf{best\_mono}): growing the log can only improve the
  best --- the ratchet;
\item once terminal, always terminal (\textsf{terminal\_ratchet}):
  the frontier never re-opens a closed question;
\item the grade ladder is strict and grade movement moves the key
  upward (\textsf{ladder\_strict}, \textsf{grade\_moves\_key} ---
  axiom-free).
\end{itemize}

\noindent
The build prints each theorem's trusted base; the ratchet lemmas
rest on \textsf{propext} and \textsf{Quot.sound} only.

\paragraph{What is asked of generated code}
Generated content is asked for proofs \emph{when appropriate and
feasible}: a proof obligation enters a manifest when the artifact is
kernel-adjacent --- a certificate schema must state (and, where
feasible, prove) its fail-safe direction; a specialization's
embedding, its exactness; a new result payload, its ordering.
Per-program translation correctness is deliberately \emph{not}
proved: that is what the square checks empirically, per run, which
is the platform's founding economy.

\paragraph{The six theorems, re-accounted}
The previous design stated six facilitation theorems over a
filtration model --- in the prior version of this paper, preserved
in the repository's history beside the era record. The redesign
keeps their intent and relocates their burdens; the accounting is the
cheapest way to see what the redesign gave up and what it kept:

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{3pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lp{15.5em}@{}}
\toprule
was & became \\
\midrule
F1 unfalsifiable map & by construction: only the kernel writes
  results; witnesses replay, universals grade, contradictions are
  recorded events \\
F2 monotone exploration & a data-structure property:
  append-only log $+$ fold (\textsf{best\_mono},
  \textsf{terminal\_ratchet}) \\
F3 complete local gradient & retired with its taxonomy: the typed
  diagnosis became the partial result's evidence, read by an LLM,
  not a derivation engine \\
F4 relative completeness & retired with its assumptions (fair
  enumeration, gate liveness); what remains is measured, not
  proved \\
F5 saturation terminates & replaced by plug-pull: no fixpoint
  claim; exit deliverables are pure functions of the log at every
  moment \\
F6 domain-genericity & became bootstrap from empty: the kernel
  ships with zero languages, and the domain kit collapses to
  \emph{write the root interpreter, pin the benchmark} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
The two retirements are deliberate, and both have the same cause:
F3 and F4 were theorems \emph{about the demand machinery} --- the
five-condition filtration and the fair enumeration of its typed
targets --- and the machinery was removed because its consumer
changed. What they bought (knowing what to build next, and that
building it suffices) is now bought empirically, by the conjecturing
LLM reading richer evidence than the taxonomy ever encoded --- a
trade of proved completeness for observed productivity that the
status of \cref{sec:status} exists to keep honest.
